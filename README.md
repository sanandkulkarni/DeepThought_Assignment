# Methodology Document

## DeepThought Business Analytics Internship Assignment

### Research Methodology – Target Company Identification & Qualification

## Overview

This document outlines the methodology I followed to identify and shortlist **25 companies** matching DeepThought’s Ideal Customer Profile (ICP), referred to in the assignment as **Federer Companies**.

The assignment was completed between **28 May and 30 May**.

The objective was to identify companies that are:

* producer-led
* operationally present in India
* growth-oriented
* differentiated in product/capability
* operationally structured
* aligned with DeepThought’s Federer scoring framework

---

# Research Objective

The main objective of this project was to build a qualified list of companies that match DeepThought’s target segment and demonstrate:

* in-house capability building
* growth signals
* technical or differentiated offerings
* systems maturity
* strong decision-maker quality
* leadership depth or succession readiness

---

# Research Process

My workflow was completed in **three stages**.

---

# Stage 1 – Building the Initial Company Universe

I first created a broad list of approximately **90 companies** within relevant target segments.

The aim at this stage was to create a large enough company universe from which the best-fit companies could be shortlisted after qualification and scoring.

---

# Stage 2 – Eligibility Filtering (E1 and E2)

After collecting the initial list, I filtered companies based on the two eligibility gates defined in the assignment.

## E1 – Producer

Verified whether the company:

* manufactures its own products
* has its own plant/facility
* has in-house production or operational infrastructure

Rejected:

* traders
* distributors
* importers
* resellers without manufacturing capability

---

## E2 – Accessible

Verified whether the company:

* has operational presence in India
* has visible physical infrastructure such as plant, factory, R&D unit, or manufacturing facility

Only companies passing **both E1 and E2** were taken forward for scoring.

---

# Stage 3 – Federer Scoring & Final Shortlisting

Companies that passed E1 and E2 were evaluated using the **Federer Score** across:

* **C3 – Differentiation**
* **C4 – Decision-Maker Quality**
* **C5 – Growing Sector**
* **C6 – Growth Signals**
* **C7 – Systems Maturity**
* **C8 – Leadership Succession**

Each company was scored using publicly available evidence.

Based on total score, companies were categorized into:

* **A – Strong Federer**
* **B – Probable Federer**
* **C – Borderline**
* **D – Not ICP**

After scoring, the list was sorted in descending order of Overall Federer Score.

The top **25 companies** were retained as the final output.

# Key Learnings from the Research

This assignment gave several practical insights about the segment.

---

## 1. Many companies initially appear industrial but are not producers

A significant number looked like manufacturers initially, but deeper research showed they were:

* traders
* distributors
* importers

This made E1 filtering critical.

---

# 2. Company websites are strong indicators of operational maturity

Companies with stronger internal systems usually had:

* better website structure
* detailed product documentation
* plant/infrastructure information
* certifications displayed
* leadership visibility

These often correlated with higher C7 scores.

---

# 3. Growth companies leave visible digital signals

High-growth companies commonly showed:

* recent updates
* hiring activity
* certifications
* expansion announcements
* updated websites
* new facilities

These became useful indicators while scoring C6.

---

# 4. Manual verification remains important

AI improved speed significantly, but manual validation was still necessary because:

* websites can be outdated
* information can be incomplete
* some companies appear as manufacturers but are not

Final scoring required judgment and verification beyond automated research.
